# How is a sample different from a population?

## Immediate answer

The population is the complete set of units about which a question is asked. A sample is the subset actually measured; the sample can be convenient without being representative.

## Exam answer pattern

State the target units, time and place; then identify how observed units were selected. Separate '50 observations are manageable' from '50 observations represent the population.'

## What to check

- Sample size does not repair a biased sampling frame.
- A census covers the defined population, not every imaginable population.

## Interpret your results

A sample statistic describes the observed 50 units. Claiming the same pattern for a broader population requires design evidence and uncertainty assessment.

## Learn more

Be explicit about whether observations are independent, repeated from the same entity, or selected because they were accessible.

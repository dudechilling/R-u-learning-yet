# What is a confounder in a comparison?

## Immediate answer

A confounder is an additional variable related to the exposure and outcome that can distort their observed association. An apparent effect may partly reflect differences in that third variable.

## Exam answer pattern

Name the exposure, outcome and plausible third variable; explain how the third variable relates to both rather than asserting a cause from correlation alone.

## What to check

- A mediator or consequence of the exposure is not automatically a confounder.
- Unmeasured confounding cannot be ruled out by a strong p-value.

## Interpret your results

A correlation between outdoor temperature and ice cream purchases need not be a direct cause; shared seasonal factors can influence both.

## Learn more

Design or adjustment may reduce some confounding, but require causal assumptions and reliable measurements.

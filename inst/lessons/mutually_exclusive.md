# Are mutually exclusive events independent?

## Immediate answer

Mutually exclusive events cannot happen together, so P(A and B)=0. Independent events satisfy P(A and B)=P(A)P(B). Two positive-probability events cannot satisfy both.

## Exam answer pattern

Explain whether the two events can both happen in one trial, then contrast that test with whether learning one changes the chance of the other.

## What to check

- A single die cannot land on two faces in one trial.
- Different trials can be independent even though each trial has mutually exclusive faces.

## Interpret your results

Rolling 2 versus rolling 5 on one fair die are mutually exclusive but dependent under the event definition.

## Learn more

Zero-probability edge cases need separate care; the simple contrast assumes both events have positive probability.
